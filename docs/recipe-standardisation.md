# Standard Waffle Recipe — Pilot Version 0.1

**Status:** Test formula only. Not yet approved as the commercial standard.

## Objective

Establish a repeatable, affordable-premium waffle with a consistent texture, flavour, portion size, and production yield. Compare butter and olive-oil versions without changing other variables.

## Base batch

The following is a controlled starting formula for a small pilot. It is not a claim about final yield; the number of waffles depends on batter portion and cooking conditions. The supplied machine is a Belgian square unit rated at 1,100 W (220–240 V, 50/60 Hz), with a stated capacity of four waffles per cooking cycle.

The business supplied current purchase quotes for flour, eggs, milk, butter, Blue Band Original, olive oil, and vanilla essence. See [ingredient cost analysis](ingredient-cost-analysis.md) for unit costs and partial batch subtotals. Sugar, baking powder, and salt prices remain missing.

Treat butter, Blue Band Original, and olive oil as separate test arms. Keep flour, sugar, baking powder, salt, eggs, milk, essence, batter portion, machine setting, and cooking endpoint constant. Do not combine the Blue Band trial with the olive-oil trial. If testing a different essence (strawberry or blueberry), do that after selecting the fat version so the comparison remains controlled.

## Method

1. Weigh and record all ingredients before mixing.
2. Preheat the waffle maker according to its manufacturer's instructions. Use the same heat setting for all comparison batches.
3. Whisk flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt together in one bowl.
4. In a second bowl, whisk eggs and milk. Whisk in cooled melted butter for the butter trial, or oil for the oil trial. Add essence.
5. Add wet ingredients to dry ingredients. Fold just until combined; a few small lumps are acceptable. Avoid prolonged mixing.
6. Rest the batter for 10 minutes, covered. Use the same rest time for every trial.
7. Weigh the batter portion used for each waffle. Use the same portion for both versions and record it.
8. Cook using the same machine setting and the same doneness standard. Record cooking time for each waffle.
9. Cool or serve using the same handling method during comparison. Record the time from cooking to tasting.
10. Clean and reset the machine consistently between batches.

If the batter is too thick or thin to portion consistently, record the observation first. Do not quietly add milk or flour; any adjustment becomes a new version and must be documented.

## Controlled comparison protocol

Run at least three batches of each fat version (butter, Blue Band Original, and olive oil) across comparable conditions. If testing the low-fat Blue Band tub, treat it as a fourth separate arm. Alternate trial order where practical to reduce machine-temperature and time-of-day bias.

Keep constant:
- Ingredient brands and quantities other than the fat product being compared.
- Egg size/weight and milk.
- Batter mixing and rest time.
- Waffle maker, heat setting, and batter portion weight.
- Cooking endpoint and post-cook holding conditions.

Record:
- Total batter weight.
- Number of waffles produced.
- Batter portion weight per waffle.
- Cooking time.
- Saleable waffles and rejects.
- Appearance, browning, crispness, interior texture, flavour, aroma, and perceived greasiness.
- Taste immediately and after the realistic planned delivery interval.
- Ingredient cost per batch and per saleable waffle.

Use a simple blind tasting code (A/B) and ask tasters to score each attribute from 1–5. Collect comments separately. Do not tell tasters which fat was used until they have scored.

## Flavour testing

After selecting the base formula, test vanilla, strawberry, and blueberry essence separately. Start with the manufacturer's recommended dosage; record exact millilitres and brand. Do not assume every essence has equal strength. Confirm the final ingredient declaration for each flavour.

## Recipe approval criteria

Approve a commercial standard only after:
- The formula produces repeatable batter and saleable yield.
- Waffle size and portion weight are consistent.
- Texture and flavour meet the intended affordable-premium positioning.
- The product remains acceptable over the actual collection/delivery interval.
- Cost per saleable waffle is known.
- Ingredient and allergen disclosures match the recipe and preparation process.
