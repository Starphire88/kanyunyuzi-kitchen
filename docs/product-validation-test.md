# Product Validation and Freshness Test

## Purpose

Validate the waffle itself and the pre-order operating promise before wider launch.

## Test A — Butter versus oil

Follow the controlled protocol in [the pilot recipe](recipe-standardisation.md). Run at least three batches per version, keep all other variables constant, and record saleable yield and cost.

## Test B — Freshness over scheduled handoff

The business model is pre-order only, with waffles prepared each morning before workplace delivery and scheduled delivery or collection. Test the actual time gap between cooking, packing, handoff, and consumption.

For each selected fat version, assess samples at:
- Immediately after cooking.
- 30 minutes after cooking.
- 60 minutes after cooking.
- 90 minutes after cooking.

These are test intervals, not a promise of product quality or a food-safety shelf-life statement. Use the real packaging intended for customers. Record whether the waffle is crisp, soft, soggy, dry, or otherwise changed. If the intended delivery interval is longer, test that interval too.

## Test C — Bundle handling

Pack the 2, 4, 6, and 8 waffle quantities using the proposed packaging. Check:
- Whether waffles are crushed or steam-softened.
- Whether flavours can be distinguished and correctly labelled.
- Whether the bundle is easy to carry.
- Whether packaging cost is commercially viable.
- Whether the product arrives in an acceptable condition.

## Test D — Customer feedback

Collect feedback on:
1. Whether the waffle was eaten warm/fresh or after cooling, and how it tasted at that temperature.
2. Whether the customer received the correct flavour.
3. What the customer liked and what should improve.

Add structured ratings only if they help compare batches; keep free-text feedback for unexpected issues.

## Decision gate

Choose a recipe and packaging combination only when taste, consistency, yield, cost, and actual delivery-window performance are all acceptable. If feedback is mixed, identify whether the issue is recipe, cooking, packaging, or time-to-consumption before changing several variables at once.
